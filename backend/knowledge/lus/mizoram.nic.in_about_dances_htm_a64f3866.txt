Mizo dances include:

1. Khuallam: performed during ceremonies like 'Khuangchawi', symbolizing bravery and wealth.
	* Accompanied by gongs, no song is sung.
	* The dance involves swaying traditional Mizo cloth (Puandum) wrapped over the shoulders.

2. Cheraw:
	* Believed to have originated in the 1st Century A.D.
	* Men tap bamboo staves on the ground with girls dancing in and out between them.
	* Gongs and drums are used to accompany the dance.

3. Sarlamkai/Solakia:
	* Originates from the Pawi and Mara communities.
	* Performed after a vanquished enemy's skull is displayed, symbolizing victory and dominance.
	* The victor is not allowed to sleep with women during the 5-day ceremony.

4. Chailam: performed on 'Chapchar Kut' festival, accompanied by drums and mithun's horn.
	* Men and women dance in circles with a musician choreographing the nuances of the dance.

5. Chawnglaizawn:
	* Mourned for a deceased wife by her husband or celebrated by successful hunters.
	* Boys and girls dance in rows to the beat of drums, using shawls to aid movement.

6. Chheihlam: originated after 1900, performed to 'Chheih hla' song with accompanying drum or bamboo tube beats.
	* Embodies joy and exhilaration, often performed over a round of rice beer in the evening.

7. Tlanglam:
	* Popular dance performed throughout Mizoram, using music from 'Puma Zai'.
	* Both sexes participate, with various variations.

8. Zangtalam: Paihte dance performed by men and women, accompanied by a drummer.
	* Dancers sing responsive songs, with the duration of the dance dependent on the drummer.

The population of Assam is a broad racial intermixture of Mongolian, Indo-Burmese, Indo-Iranian, and Aryan origin. The state's native language is called Assamese, and it is the principal language of the state.

Assamese-speaking Hindus represent two-thirds of the state's population, while indigenous Tibeto-Burman tribal groups make up 16 percent of the total. More than 40 percent of Assam's population is thought to be of migrant origin.

The Ahom dynasty ruled Assam for six hundred years and managed to keep the kingdom independent from invaders before the British arrived in 1824. The majority of the Assamese are Vaishnavas, a sect of Hinduism that does not believe in idol worshiping.

Assam has a rich cultural life with two important cultural and religious institutions: satras (the seat of a religious head) and naamghar (the house of names). Weaving is an ancient tradition among the Assamese women, producing silk and cotton cloths of various designs.

The state celebrates three Bihu festivals with great enthusiasm, regardless of caste, creed, or religion. The population is also known for its handicrafts, including weaving, woodwork, and metalwork.
